# Report 4 — our response defs vs `objectiveai-api`'s claude_agent_sdk port

Two independent Rust ports of "what Claude Code's stream says,"
diffed both ways. Load-bearing claims spot-verified in both trees.

## The framing fact: two different wires

The comparison is only meaningful with this in view. **We** model
`claude -p --output-format stream-json --verbose` stdout directly.
**The api crate never reads that wire**: it spawns a Python runner
wrapping the Python `claude-agent-sdk` and parses the RUNNER's
envelope (`{"type":"event","id":…,"event":<SDKMessage>}`) — the
Python SDK's dataclasses re-serialized, one indirection away from the
CLI. Several "differences" below are each side being right about its
own wire. The api crate is also being REPLACED by this work, so the
purpose here is salvage: what did they learn that we should take?

## What they solved that we should consider taking

1. **The newer-SDK API surface, wholesale.** Their port is from a
   much newer `@anthropic-ai/sdk` and closes most of our report-3
   item 3: `BetaContentBlock` has 15 variants (ours 4) — the eleven
   we lack (`server_tool_use`, `web_search_tool_result`,
   `web_fetch_tool_result`, `code_execution_tool_result`,
   `bash_code_execution_tool_result`,
   `text_editor_code_execution_tool_result`,
   `tool_search_tool_result`, `mcp_tool_use`, `mcp_tool_result`,
   `container_upload`, `compaction`) exist fully typed, with their
   nested content/error unions, ready to port. Likewise: 5 citation
   kinds (+`file_id` on the shared three), a 6th content-block delta
   (`compaction_delta`), a typed `BetaContainer`/`BetaSkill` and
   `BetaContextManagementResponse` (which our `Option<Value>`
   `container`/`context_management` could adopt), typed `iterations`
   usage, `caller` on tool-use blocks, and 9 extra request-side param
   blocks (their `tool_result` content admits 5 block kinds to our 2).
2. **`message_delta` carries more than we model.** Their delta event
   has `context_management` on the event and `container` on the
   delta; their `BetaMessageDeltaUsage` has six fields to our ONE
   (`output_tokens`) — we silently drop five usage fields from every
   `message_delta` today. This is our report-1-class "usage" gap in
   a second place, and worth fixing regardless of the block question.
3. **`cache_control` has a `ttl`.** Theirs is a struct
   `{type, ttl: Option<"5m"|"1h">}` (verified); ours is a bare
   `{type}` enum that would DROP a ttl on round-trip. Their shape is
   the newer SDK's; ours should grow the field.
4. **Evidence of two rate-limit statuses we don't name.** Their
   `RateLimitStatus` includes `queueing` and `queueing_soft`
   (verified). Our open tail means we don't break — they land in
   `Other` — but they're worth promoting to named variants.
5. **A real-wire regression fixture.** Their tests carry a
   content-censored REAL tool-result line (no `session_id`, no
   `parent_tool_use_id`, array-valued `tool_use_result`,
   string-content user message). Checked against our types: we parse
   both fixtures cleanly — our merged `User` design is validated by
   their bug evidence (their `session_id` was widened to `Option`
   AFTER silent drops ate real tool results in production).

## What we solved that they haven't (their latent bugs, our lessons)

6. **Open tails.** Every string vocabulary of theirs is a closed
   enum — `BetaStopReason` (8, no `Other`), `ServiceTier`
   (`standard|priority|batch`), `Speed` (`standard|fast`),
   `RateLimitStatus`, `ApiKeySource` (the zod five only — they do
   NOT know the producer's `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`/`apiKeyHelper`/
   `/login managed key`/`none`), `PermissionMode` (five — no `auto`,
   no `bubble`). Any new server value kills the line.
7. **…and their failure mode is a SILENT drop.** Their reader is
   `match serde_json::from_str(line) { Err(_) => return }` — no
   catch-all, no log. "Unknown types are skipped" actually means
   "unparseable LINES are skipped": a known record with one
   unexpected field value vanishes indistinguishably from a novel
   record, envelope id and all. Their own `session_id: Option`
   comment documents production data loss from exactly this.
8. **Their `SDKUserMessageReplay` is unreachable** (verified): in
   their untagged union `UserMessage` precedes it and requires only
   `type`+`message`, so every replay line matches the plain variant
   and the `isReplay` flag is silently lost — their struct-split is
   worse than our merged struct with `is_replay` on it.
9. **Required-dense usage.** Their result usage is
   `NonNullableBetaUsage` — all ten fields required, non-nullable —
   so a sparse usage object loses the whole cost chunk (silently,
   per 7). Ours defaults the six extras. Their nested counters
   (`server_tool_use`, `cache_creation`) are also required-undefaulted
   where ours carry `#[serde(default)]`.
10. **Coverage.** They model 18 record kinds; we model the full
    stdout union — they lack `post_turn_summary`, `api_retry`,
    `local_command_output`, `task_progress`,
    `session_state_changed`, `elicitation_complete`, `bridge_state`,
    `prompt_suggestion`, `keep_alive`, both streamlined records, and
    the ENTIRE control protocol + 27 hook inputs. They also lack
    init's `messaging_socket_path` and `plugins[].source`,
    `compact_boundary.preserved_segment`, `task_started`'s
    `workflow_name`/`prompt`, `user`'s `priority`/`timestamp`, and
    the whole overage sub-model of `rate_limit_info`. They terminate
    on the runner's `end` line and have no concept of
    `session_state_changed{idle}` as the true turn-over signal.

## Unresolved questions

- **Numeric convention: their uniform `i64` vs our `u64`.** Counts
  are non-negative, so ours is the tighter model; but `return_code`
  in the code-execution blocks is genuinely signed, and if we port
  their eleven blocks we inherit that decision per field. Also their
  `duration_ms: i64` vs our `f64` (ours is right — `Date.now()`
  deltas are integral but the schema says number and jitter exists
  elsewhere) and their `total_cost_usd: Decimal` vs our `f64`
  (Decimal is arguably better for money; adopting it would add a
  dependency and ripple into the SDK's chunk types).
- **Null-emit vs key-omit on optional params.** We emit `null` for
  the optional-and-nullable fields; they skip every optional key.
  Both parse each other's output; only one can be byte-faithful to
  the CLI's own emissions, and neither tree has a captured CLI line
  proving which. (Their fixtures are RUNNER lines.)
- **`{"type":"rate_limit"}`.** They accept it as a terminal
  bare-payload record; we would reject it. It carries no
  uuid/session_id, which suggests their Python runner synthesizes
  it — in which case both sides are right about their own wire and
  we should NOT add it. Unverifiable without the runner source.
- **`file_id` on citations: response-only?** Their response
  citations carry it, their param citations don't — internal
  asymmetry in their tree, needs a d.ts check at their SDK version
  before we copy either reading.
- **`server_tool_use.input` as `IndexMap<String,Value>`** (their
  only non-`Value` tool input) — deliberate ordered-object choice or
  accident? If we port the block, we must pick.
- **Their param `role: user|assistant` vs our `user`-only.** Right
  for the API params type; for CC's stdout user records our
  evidence says `user` only. If we ever port their param blocks
  wholesale, keep our narrower role.
- **`ApiKeySource`/`PermissionMode` value sets** remain without a
  captured-line proof on either side; our supersets are strictly
  safer and cost nothing. Their closed sets surviving production
  proves little given 7 — a permanently-dropped init line is
  invisible to them.

## Net judgment

Their tree's value to us is the NEWER SDK SURFACE (items 1–4): a
ready-made, typed port of everything our pinned 0.39 d.ts predates.
Their reading of ROBUSTNESS is behind ours everywhere it matters —
closed vocabularies, silent whole-line drops, an unreachable
variant, required-dense usage — and two of their bugs (8, 9) are
direct validations of choices we made differently. If we adopt their
block vocabulary, we should port the SHAPES but keep our doctrine:
open tails on API-owned vocabularies, defaults on counters, markers
required, and loud failure at the line level.
