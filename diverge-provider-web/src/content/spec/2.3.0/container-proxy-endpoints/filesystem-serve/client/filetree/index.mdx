The client opens this channel to watch the served subtree.

- **The request.** The payload is the tag byte `10` and nothing more.
  [Request](/container-proxy-endpoints/filesystem-serve/client/filetree/request/) states its form.
- **The response.** A snapshot, one channel response per change, and
  the channel response finish when the scope ends.
  [Response](/container-proxy-endpoints/filesystem-serve/client/filetree/response/) states the sequence.
- **As a tree scope.** The server watches and walks the subtree as
  [filesystem::tree](/container-proxy-endpoints/filesystem-tree/) watches and walks the
  container: the watch armed before the walk, the snapshot first, one
  channel response per change in the order the changes were observed,
  a directory the server could not watch present with `changes` `0`
  and no change under it sent, and lost events answered by a fresh
  snapshot. The root of the tree is the directory the request named,
  and every `path` is a sequence of components from that root.
- **Left out.** `/proc`, `/sys` and `/dev` are left out of every
  subtree, as of every tree. No other path is left out: the paths a
  tree scope's request names do not apply to a serve.
- **Every channel is whole.** Each filetree channel begins with its
  own snapshot. Two filetree channels on one scope are two streams,
  and each reports every change.
- **Ended with the scope.** The channel response finish of a filetree
  channel is sent when the scope ends, after the last change. The
  client ends a filetree channel by ending the scope, and not
  otherwise.
