---
title: 'Credits'
summary: 'Every stream is paced by its reader: a sender sends one frame ahead, and the reader returns room for the next with a credit frame when it takes one, or abandons the stream.'
order: 5
draft: false
---

Every stream on a connection is paced by the party that reads it. A
sender holds room for exactly one frame on each stream it sends. The
reader returns that room with a **credit** frame (type `7`) each time
it takes a frame off the stream, and a reader that will read no more
says so with a credit frame of its own.

```text
[type: 7][scope: u32, big-endian][channel: u32, big-endian][kind: u8]
```

- **The streams.** A stream is one of the following, in either
  direction:
  - a scope's responses (types `2`), sent by the server;
  - a scope's channel requests (type `4`) from one party, the stream
    of every channel that party opens in the scope;
  - one channel's responses (type `5`), sent by its responder.
- **The kind.** The payload is exactly one byte:

| Kind | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `0` | room for one more frame on the response stream the header names |
| `1` | room for one more channel request in the scope |
| `2` | the reader reads nothing more of the response stream the header names |
| `3` | the reader reads nothing more of the scope's channel requests |

- **Naming the stream.** The scope field names the scope. For kinds
  `0` and `2`, the channel field is `0` for the scope's own responses
  and otherwise the channel number as its opener chose it; the reader
  of a channel's responses is that channel's opener, so it quotes its
  own number. For kinds `1` and `3`, the channel field is `0`, and the
  stream is the channel requests the receiver of the credit sends in
  the scope.
- **Opening room.** Every stream opens with room for exactly one
  frame: a scope's responses with its request, a scope's channel
  requests from each party with the scope's request, and a channel's
  responses with its channel request.
- **Sending spends room.** A party sends a response, a channel request
  or a channel response only while it holds room on that stream, and
  each such frame spends one unit of room. A party without room on a
  stream sends nothing on that stream until a credit of kind `0` or
  `1` gives it room. Waiting on one stream delays no other stream.
- **Finishes need no room.** A response finish (type `3`) and a
  channel response finish (type `6`) spend no room and are sent
  whether or not room is held. A reader therefore holds at most one
  unread frame and one finish on each stream.
- **Room returns when a frame is taken.** A reader sends a credit of
  kind `0` or `1` when it takes a frame off a stream to act on it, and
  not when the frame arrives. A finish returns no room.
- **Abandoning.** A reader that will read no more of a stream before
  its finish sends a credit of kind `2` or `3`, exactly once. From that
  credit on, the sender sends no response, channel request or channel
  response on that stream, and the reader discards any such frame that
  was already on its way. Abandoning ends neither a scope nor a
  channel: only a finish ends either, and the responder still sends
  it.
- **A frame without room ends the connection.** A party that receives
  a response, a channel request or a channel response on a stream to
  which it gave no room closes the connection.
- **A credit for an unknown stream is disregarded.** A credit naming
  a scope that is not open, or a stream that has ended, changes
  nothing and does not end the connection.
- **Endings end the room.** When a scope finishes, every stream within
  it ends, and room held on any of them is void. When the connection
  ends, every stream on it ends.
- **Opening a scope is not paced.** A request (type `1`) spends no
  room. A server reads requests at its own pace.

**Remark.** The rules bound what any party holds for any stream to one
frame and one finish, whatever the speed of either party: a frame is
at most one message, and no sender runs further ahead of its reader
than the frame the reader is acting on.
