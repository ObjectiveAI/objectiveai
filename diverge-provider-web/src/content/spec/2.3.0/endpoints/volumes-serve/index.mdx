A client opens the scope with a request whose payload is the tag byte
`8` followed by the name of a volume and `overlay_disk`. The server
sends exactly one response, which is either `Serving` or an error.
After `Serving` the scope stays open: the client opens on it, as
channels, the nine asks a FUSE mount makes of a client — a stat, a
read of a piece, a write of a piece, a truncate, a setattr, a listing,
a removal, a rename and a mkdir — and the server answers each from
the volume, as the volume's mode allows. The client's stop ends the
scope: the response finish follows the last answer, and no frame
follows the finish. After an error the response finish follows the
error, and no channel is served.

- **The request.** The request carries the name of one volume and
  `overlay_disk`, the cap of the layer an ephemeral volume is served
  on. [Request](/endpoints/volumes-serve/request/) states its form.
- **The volume named.** The client names a volume by the `name` its
  [volumes::list](/endpoints/volumes-list/) listing gave it. The
  server resolves the name against the identity under which the
  connection was authorized, as [Authorization](/authorization/)
  provides. A name that is not in the client's listing is answered
  with an error. A client cannot name another client's volume.
- **The response.** [Response](/endpoints/volumes-serve/response/) states the one response
  and what follows it.
- **The channels.** The client opens the channels that
  [Client Channels](/endpoints/volumes-serve/client/) defines: the nine asks, and the
  stop. The server opens no channel on a serve scope.
- **A served volume is a mounted volume.** The server holds the volume
  as a running container that mounts it holds it, from the moment it
  sends `Serving` until the response finish. While it holds it, a
  `volumes::stat`, `volumes::read`, `volumes::write`,
  `volumes::filetree`, `volumes::edit` or `volumes::delete` naming
  the volume is answered with an error. A volume that is under one of
  those six at the time of the request is not served: the request is
  answered with an error.
- **Who may hold a served volume.** The volume's `mode`, as the
  listing reports it, says. An `ephemeral` or a `read_only` volume
  may be held by any number of serves and any number of running
  containers at once. A `persistent` volume has exactly one user at a
  time: a serve of it is refused with an error while any running
  container mounts it or another serve holds it, and a run naming it
  is refused while a serve holds it, as
  [containers::agents::run](/endpoints/containers-agents-run/)
  states.
- **The asks are the mount's.** Each ask is, byte for byte, the ask a
  FUSE mount's scope carries on the container proxy, with no id: the
  scope is the volume, as the mount scope is the mount. Each answer is
  the answer that ask defines. A client that bridges a FUSE mount on
  one provider to a volume on another relays each ask and each answer
  as it is.
- **Every piece crosses as it is.** A read is one piece at an offset,
  a write lands one piece in place, and a stat is the entry's kind,
  size, mode, owner, group and times as the volume records them at the
  time of the answer. The server buffers no content of the volume on
  the client's behalf: what a write asks for is in the volume, or in
  the serve's layer, when the answer is `ok`.
- **Asks in flight at once.** A client may have any number of asks open
  at once. The server answers each on its own channel, in whatever
  order it answers them.
- **The three modes.** A `persistent` volume is changed in place:
  every mutation the server answers `ok` is in the volume. An
  `ephemeral` volume is served on a layer of the serve's own: the
  serve starts from the volume as it is, every mutation the server
  answers `ok` is in the layer and is seen by every later ask of the
  same serve, nothing of the layer is in the volume, and the layer is
  discarded at the finish; the layer holds at most `overlay_disk`
  bytes, a mutation that would need room past that is answered with
  an error and the volume and the layer are as they were, and the
  serve continues. A `read_only` volume answers every stat, read and
  listing as any volume does, and answers every write, truncate,
  setattr, removal, rename and mkdir with the read-only byte: the
  change is refused, and the volume is as it was.
- **The stop.** The client ends the scope by opening the stop channel.
  The server answers every ask still open, sends the response finish,
  and gives the volume back. The server sends nothing on the stop's
  own channel. A connection that closes with a serve scope open ends
  the scope the same way: the server gives the volume back.
- **The error.** An error states that the volume is not served, and
  that nothing was determined about the volume. A volume absent from
  the client's listing, a volume under a `volumes::stat`,
  `volumes::read`, `volumes::write`, `volumes::filetree`,
  `volumes::edit` or `volumes::delete` at the time of the request, a
  `persistent` volume mounted in a running container or held by
  another serve at the time of the request, an `ephemeral` volume
  whose `overlay_disk` the server cannot set aside, and a volume the
  server could not open are each answered with an error.
