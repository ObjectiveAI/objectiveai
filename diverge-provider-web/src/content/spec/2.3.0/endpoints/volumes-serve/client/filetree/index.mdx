The client opens this channel to watch the served volume as this serve
sees it.

- **The request.** The payload is the tag byte `9` and nothing more.
  [Request](/endpoints/volumes-serve/client/filetree/request/) states its form.
- **The response.** A snapshot, one channel response per change, and
  the channel response finish when the scope ends.
  [Response](/endpoints/volumes-serve/client/filetree/response/) states the sequence.
- **The tree is the serve's view.** The snapshot is the volume as this
  serve sees it: for a `persistent` volume the volume; for an
  `ephemeral` volume the volume under the layer of this serve, so an
  entry the layer holds is in the tree as the layer holds it; for a
  `read_only` volume the volume. The root of the tree is the root of
  the volume.
- **What changes.** A change is a mutation this serve answered `ok`: a
  write, a truncate, a setattr, a mkdir, a removal or a rename on one
  of this scope's channels. Nothing else changes what this serve sees,
  and nothing else is reported. A `read_only` volume answers no
  mutation `ok`, and its stream is the snapshot and nothing after it.
- **Every channel is whole.** Each filetree channel begins with its
  own snapshot. Two filetree channels on one scope are two streams,
  and each reports every change.
- **Every directory is watched.** Every `Directory` in the snapshot
  has `changes` `1`: every change beneath it is reported, since every
  change passes through this serve.
- **Ended with the scope.** The channel response finish of a filetree
  channel is sent when the scope ends, after the last change. The
  client ends a filetree channel by ending the scope, and not
  otherwise.
- **Not a volumes::filetree.** A [volumes::filetree](/endpoints/volumes-filetree/)
  of a served volume is answered with an error while the serve holds
  the volume, and would show the volume without the layer. This
  channel is the one view of the tree of a served volume.
