The client opens this channel to perform on the served volume what a
program in a container performs on a FUSE mount. The ask names the
entry by its path within the volume, and names no volume: the scope
is the volume. It is, byte for byte, the ask of tag `7` a FUSE
mount's scope carries on the container proxy, and the server answers
it as a client answers that ask, from the volume.

- **The request.** The tag byte `7` followed by the ask.
  [Request](/endpoints/volumes-serve/client/truncate/request/) states its form.
- **The response.** Exactly one channel response and the channel
  response finish. [Response](/endpoints/volumes-serve/client/truncate/response/) states the
  sequence.
- **The meaning.** What the ask means, and what its answer means,
  [FUSE truncate](/container-proxy-endpoints/fuse-mount/server/truncate/) states for the mount, and
  [FUSE truncate](/endpoints/containers-agents-run/server/fuse-truncate/) states
  for the relay; the same holds here, the volume standing where the
  client's storage stands there.
- **The volume answers.** The server answers from the volume as it is
  at the time of the answer, and changes the volume as the ask asks
  before it answers `ok`. Nothing is buffered on the client's behalf.
- **The mode.** A `persistent` volume takes the change in place. An `ephemeral` volume takes it into the layer of this serve, and answers with an error a change that would take the layer past `overlay_disk`, the volume and the layer as they were. A `read_only` volume answers this ask with the read-only byte, and is not changed.
