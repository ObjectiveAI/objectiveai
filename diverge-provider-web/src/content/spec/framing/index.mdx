---
title: 'Framing'
summary: 'Every frame is a fixed nine-byte header — type, scope, channel — followed by a payload, and nothing is ever acknowledged.'
order: 3
---

Every message begins with nine bytes: a one-byte frame type, a
four-byte big-endian scope number, and a four-byte big-endian channel
number, followed by the payload those numbers give meaning to. Each
direction defines five frame types — an auth frame, a request that
opens a scope, responses and their finish, and channel traffic — and
no frame is ever acknowledged: delivery is the transport's job, and
the protocol's answers are frames of its own.
