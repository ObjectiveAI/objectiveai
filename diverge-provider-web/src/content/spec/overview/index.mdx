The Diverge Provider Protocol is the wire protocol between **callers**
and **providers**. A caller asks a provider to run things on its
behalf — an agentic loop, an MCP plugin, a laboratory — and to manage
the storage and images those things need. A provider supplies the
machines, the containers, and the answers. Everything travels over one
WebSocket connection, framed and multiplexed, with an authorization
handshake in front.

## The crate is the normative artifact

The protocol is defined by the
[`diverge-provider-sdk`](https://docs.rs/diverge-provider-sdk) Rust
crate, the way the Model Context Protocol is defined by its schema:
the crate's types define the messages, their encodings, and their
documented semantics. **Where this specification's prose and the crate
disagree, the crate is correct** — this site is the protocol's
readable form, not its source of truth.

That rule is also why revisions here are crate versions. This
specification's revision number is the version of the crate it
describes, read from the crate's own manifest when the site is built.
There is no separate versioning to keep in step.

## The layers

The specification is organized as layers, from the socket upward:

1. **Transport** — one WebSocket connection; either side may dial.
2. **Framing** — a fixed nine-byte header on every frame; nothing is
   acknowledged.
3. **Authorization** — the connection's first frame is a credential,
   presented by whichever side dialled.
4. **Scopes and channels** — how one connection multiplexes many
   requests, and who may end what.
5. **Endpoints** — the eleven things a caller can open a scope for.
6. **Shared exchanges** — the payloads several endpoints are built
   from: MCP, OCI, container file operations, and filesystem trees.
7. **Capabilities** — the traits each side implements: what a caller
   supplies to answer a provider, and what a provider supplies to
   serve a caller.
