# Image prompt: two daemons, one tool between them, three providers underneath

An image prompt for one picture of what landed on 2026-10-10: a tool
one daemon runs, joined by an agent of another daemon through a
provider, every container of either daemon powered by one of three
providers. The picture is a wall, not a diagram: the system drawn the
way a conspiracy theorist pins it to a corkboard, and the way an
anarchist stencils it on a shutter. Almost no text. The structure
carries the meaning.

## Prompt

A tall poster, portrait, in a hand-made anarchist / conspiracy-theory
style: xeroxed zine texture, black spray-paint stencil edges, red
string pinned between things with real pins, torn newsprint scraps,
smudged photocopier grain, a corkboard-and-concrete background, white
chalk and red marker over black. Collage, not vector. Nothing is
clean; everything is deliberate. No people, no faces, no logos.
Lettering is stencil or scrawled marker, and there is very little of
it.

**Across the very top, the only sentence in the picture, stencilled
in white spray paint, slightly misregistered: `DO NOT BE AFRAID.`**

The poster is cut into three horizontal bands by two ragged strips of
black duct tape running edge to edge.

### Band one, the top: DAEMON ALPHA

A torn label in the band's upper-left corner reads `DAEMON α`, the
word stencilled and the alpha drawn large in red marker, circled
twice as if it mattered.

Three entities sit in a row across the band, equal in size, drawn as
the same shape: a square photograph of a locked steel cabinet, each
with a stencilled codename across its door. From left to right:

1. An agent, codename `RED HERRING`.
2. An agent, codename `DEAD DROP`.
3. A tool, codename `THE ARCHIVE`. The same cabinet, but its door is
   stamped with a small gear glyph instead of an eye glyph, which is
   the one thing that tells an agent from a tool anywhere in the
   picture: agents carry a stencilled eye, tools carry a gear.

Under each AGENT hang its dependency tools: three under `RED
HERRING`, two under `DEAD DROP`. Each dependency tool is a small
thing, a third the size of the cabinets, drawn as a key tag or a
luggage tag with a gear scratched into it and no name, only a short
hex fragment in tiny monospace (`3f9a`, `b17c`, `e02d`, `77a1`,
`c5f0`). A short straight red string runs from the bottom of each
agent's cabinet down to each of its own tags, pinned at both ends.
The tags belong to their agent and to nothing else.

`DEAD DROP`, the second agent, is also attached to `THE ARCHIVE`: a
thicker red string, pinned at both cabinets, with a small paper tag
hanging off its midpoint that reads only `attached`.

### Band two, the middle: DAEMON BETA

A torn label in the band's upper-left corner reads `DAEMON β`, the
beta drawn large in red marker and circled the same way.

One agent, codename `CASSANDRA`, the same locked-cabinet shape and
size as the three above. Under it hang three dependency tools, the
same small tags, the same short red strings (`a9e4`, `0b6c`, `d18f`).

`CASSANDRA` is also joined to `THE ARCHIVE` in band one: a long red
string climbs from her cabinet up through the duct tape into the
first band and pins into `THE ARCHIVE`'s cabinet. Where the string
crosses the tape a small scrap of newsprint is pinned over it reading
only `exposed`. This string is the one line in the picture that
crosses a band border between the daemons, and it is the thing the
poster is about.

### Band three, the bottom: PROVIDERS

A torn label in the band's upper-left corner reads `PROVIDERS`.

Three providers sit across the bottom of the poster, drawn as three
different pieces of heavy infrastructure photographed in grainy
black and white and pasted down: a substation transformer, a bank of
rack servers, a radio mast. Each is stamped with a stencilled number
and nothing else: `I`, `II`, `III`.

From every agent, every tool and every dependency tool in the two
bands above, exactly one red string runs down into this band and
pins into exactly one provider. Nine entities above in band one
(two agents, one tool, five dependency tags) plus four in band two
(one agent, three dependency tags): thirteen strings, thirteen pins,
one per entity, no entity with two, no entity with none. Which
provider each string reaches is deliberately scrambled: an agent
and its own dependency tools go to different providers; the two
agents of daemon alpha go to different providers; `THE ARCHIVE` goes
to a provider that neither `DEAD DROP` nor `CASSANDRA` goes to; a
tag on the far left runs all the way to the mast on the far right,
a tag on the far right runs to the transformer on the far left. The
strings cross and recross one another in a dense red web across the
whole lower half of the poster. They are chaotic to look at and
simple to trace: every one has one start and one end.

### Everything else

- All strings are the same red, the dependency-tool strings short
  and the provider strings long. Nothing else connects anything.
- Pins are real push-pins, drawn with a shadow. Where many strings
  pin into one provider the pins cluster.
- Under a few cabinets a scrap of masking tape carries a scrawled
  marker note too small to read, as if someone annotated the wall
  and did not want it read from a distance.
- Texture everywhere: fold creases, a coffee ring on the second
  band, staple marks at the corners, a corner torn off and left
  hanging.
- No other words appear. No legend, no title besides the sentence
  at the top, no arrows with labels. The eye glyph, the gear glyph,
  the three labels, the codenames, the hex fragments, the two tags
  on strings, and the three numerals are the whole vocabulary.

## What the picture states, for whoever reads it against the system

- Two daemons hold their own agents and tools. Neither daemon
  appears as a thing; a daemon is a band, the space its containers
  stand in.
- A dependency tool belongs to one agent and lives as long as that
  agent runs: the short strings, the small tags, no names.
- An agent attached to a tool of its own daemon: `DEAD DROP` and
  `THE ARCHIVE`, the `attached` tag.
- A tool reached from another daemon: `CASSANDRA` joins `THE
  ARCHIVE` through an exposure, the one string that crosses the
  tape, the `exposed` scrap. Daemon beta does not run that tool and
  does not see its image; it holds a line to it.
- Every container, of either daemon, runs on exactly one provider,
  and which one is decided container by container, never daemon by
  daemon: the scrambled web at the bottom. A daemon has no provider
  of its own; its containers each have one.
